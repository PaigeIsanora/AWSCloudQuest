☁6 Advantages of Cloud Computing

1. Trade Capital Expense for Variable Expense

Instead of investing in data centers before you know how you'll use them, pay only when you consume computing resources and only for how much you consume.

Example: A startup launches an app without buying any servers. They pay $50/month on AWS instead of $50,000 upfront for hardware.

2. Benefit from Massive Economies of Scale

Because AWS aggregates usage from hundreds of thousands of customers, they achieve higher economies of scale, translating into lower pay-as-you-go prices.

Example: AWS can buy hardware at bulk rates far cheaper than any single company, passing savings to you as lower per-hour prices.

3. Stop Guessing Capacity

Eliminate guessing about infrastructure capacity needs. Scale up or down within minutes as demand changes, rather than sitting on idle or insufficient resources.

Example: An e-commerce site auto-scales from 2 servers to 20 during Black Friday, then back to 2 on Tuesday. No waste, no crashes.

4. Increase Speed and Agility

New IT resources are a click away, reducing the time to make resources available from weeks to minutes. This dramatically increases agility for experimentation.

Example: A developer spins up a test environment in 5 minutes, experiments, then deletes it. No procurement tickets, no waiting.

5. Stop Spending Money Running Data Centers

Focus on projects that differentiate your business, not the undifferentiated heavy lifting of racking, stacking, and powering servers.

Example: A healthcare company spends time building patient features instead of managing cooling systems, physical security, and cabling.

6. Go Global in Minutes

Easily deploy your application in multiple AWS Regions around the world. Provide lower latency and a better experience to customers at minimal cost.

Example: A gaming company deploys in US, EU, and Asia-Pacific regions so players everywhere get <50ms latency.

🏗AWS Well-Architected Framework (6 Pillars)

1. Operational Excellence

Run and monitor systems to deliver business value. Continuously improve supporting processes and procedures. Automate changes, respond to events, and define standards.

Example: Using CloudFormation to deploy infrastructure as code, and CloudWatch alarms to auto-detect and respond to issues without manual intervention.

2. Security

Protect information, systems, and assets through risk assessments and mitigation strategies. Confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data.

Example: Enabling MFA on all IAM users, encrypting S3 data with KMS, and using CloudTrail to log every API call for auditing.

3. Reliability

Ensure a workload performs its intended function correctly and consistently. Recover from failures, meet demand, and mitigate disruptions.

Example: Deploying an app across 3 Availability Zones with Auto Scaling, so if one AZ goes down, traffic shifts to the others automatically.

4. Performance Efficiency

Use computing resources efficiently to meet requirements. Maintain efficiency as demand changes and technologies evolve.

Example: Using Lambda for sporadic batch jobs (no idle servers) and CloudFront CDN to cache static assets at edge locations near users.

5. Cost Optimization

Avoid unnecessary costs. Understand and control where money is being spent. Select the right number and type of resources.

Example: Purchasing Reserved Instances for steady-state workloads (72% savings), using Spot Instances for fault-tolerant batch processing.

6. Sustainability

Minimize environmental impacts of running cloud workloads. Reduce energy consumption and increase efficiency through right-sizing and managed services.

Example: Using Graviton (ARM) instances that use less energy, deleting unused EBS snapshots, and right-sizing over-provisioned instances.

🚀AWS Cloud Adoption Framework (CAF)

The CAF organizes guidance into 6 Perspectives grouped into two focus areas. It helps organizations plan and execute their cloud adoption journey.

Business-Focused Perspectives

Business-Focused

Business Perspective

Ensures IT aligns with business needs and cloud investments link to key business results. Covers strategy management, portfolio management, innovation, and product management.

Stakeholders: CEO, CFO, COO, CIO, business managers
Business-Focused

People Perspective

Bridges technology and business by evolving culture and organizational structure. Covers change management, training, career development, and transformation leadership.

Stakeholders: CIO, COO, HR directors, cloud directors, cross-functional leaders
Business-Focused

Governance Perspective

Orchestrate cloud initiatives while maximizing benefits and minimizing risk. Covers program/project management, budgeting, risk management, benefits measurement, and licensing.

Stakeholders: CIO, CTO, CFO, CDO, enterprise architects, PMOs

Technology-Focused Perspectives

Tech-Focused

Platform Perspective

Build an enterprise-grade cloud platform, modernize existing workloads, and implement new solutions. Covers platform architecture, data architecture, provisioning, and CI/CD.

Stakeholders: CTO, IT managers, solutions architects, DevOps engineers
Tech-Focused

Security Perspective

Achieve confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data and workloads. Covers IAM, threat detection, vulnerability management, infrastructure security, and compliance.

Stakeholders: CISO, CCO, IT security analysts, security architects
Tech-Focused

Operations Perspective

Ensure cloud services are delivered at agreed-upon levels. Covers event management, incident management, change management, performance monitoring, and continuity.

Stakeholders: IT operations managers, site reliability engineers, IT support managers

Transformation Domains & Phases

4 Transformation Domains

Technology — migrate and modernize infrastructure, applications, data/analytics platforms. Process — digitize, automate, and optimize business operations. Organization — reimagine operating model and how teams organize around value. Product — reimagine business model by creating new value propositions.

Example: A retail company migrates their data warehouse (Technology), automates inventory reorders (Process), creates cross-functional cloud teams (Organization), and launches a data-as-a-service product (Product).

4 Transformation Phases

Envision — demonstrate how cloud accelerates business outcomes, identify key stakeholders. Align — identify capability gaps across the 6 perspectives, identify cross-org dependencies. Launch — deliver pilot initiatives, generate business value. Scale — expand production pilots, scale to full desired business value.

Example: A bank envisions cloud-based real-time fraud detection, aligns security and compliance requirements, launches a pilot for one card product, then scales to all payment channels.

💻Compute Services

Compute

Amazon EC2

Elastic Compute Cloud. Resizable virtual servers (instances) in the cloud. You choose instance type, OS, and pay per second/hour.

Example: Running a Node.js web server on a t3.micro instance to host a company website.
Compute

AWS Lambda

Serverless compute. Run code in response to events without provisioning servers. Pay only for compute time consumed (per ms).

Example: A function that resizes uploaded images automatically when they land in an S3 bucket.
Compute

AWS Elastic Beanstalk

PaaS. Upload your code and it handles capacity provisioning, load balancing, auto-scaling, and health monitoring.

Example: Deploying a Python Flask app by just uploading a ZIP file — Beanstalk configures everything.
Compute

Amazon ECS

Elastic Container Service. Fully managed container orchestration (Docker). AWS-native alternative to Kubernetes.

Example: Running 10 microservices as Docker containers managed by ECS with auto-scaling.
Compute

Amazon EKS

Elastic Kubernetes Service. Managed Kubernetes for teams already using K8s. AWS handles the control plane.

Example: Migrating an existing on-prem Kubernetes cluster to AWS without rewriting deployment configs.
Compute

AWS Fargate

Serverless compute engine for containers. Works with ECS/EKS. No need to manage underlying EC2 instances.

Example: Running containers without worrying about patching, scaling, or managing the host servers.
Compute

Amazon Lightsail

Simplified compute for small projects. Fixed monthly pricing, bundled with storage, networking, and DNS. Easy for beginners.

Example: Hosting a WordPress blog for $5/month with predictable pricing.
Compute

AWS Outposts

Run AWS infrastructure on-premises. Same hardware and APIs as the cloud but in your own data center for low-latency or data residency needs.

Example: A factory needs <5ms latency for machine learning inference, so they run it on an Outpost rack on-site.

📦Storage Services

Storage

Amazon S3

Simple Storage Service. Object storage with 11 nines (99.999999999%) durability. Unlimited storage, files up to 5TB. Multiple storage classes for cost control.

Example: Storing user-uploaded photos, hosting a static website, or keeping data lake files for analytics.
Storage

S3 Storage Classes

Standard (frequent access), Standard-IA (infrequent, fast retrieval), One Zone-IA (single AZ), Intelligent-Tiering (auto-moves), Glacier Instant/Flexible/Deep Archive (archival).

Example: Moving 7-year-old compliance logs to Glacier Deep Archive at $1/TB/month instead of $23/TB on Standard.
Storage

Amazon EBS

Elastic Block Store. Persistent block storage volumes for EC2 instances. Like a virtual hard drive. Snapshots for backup.

Example: Attaching a 100GB SSD volume to an EC2 instance running a database that needs persistent storage.
Storage

Amazon EFS

Elastic File System. Managed NFS file system. Multiple EC2 instances can access the same files simultaneously. Auto-scales.

Example: 10 web servers sharing the same content directory so file uploads are instantly visible from all servers.
Storage

AWS Storage Gateway

Hybrid cloud storage. Connects on-premises environments to cloud storage. Caches frequently accessed data locally.

Example: An office file server that automatically backs up to S3 while keeping recent files cached locally for fast access.
Storage

AWS Snow Family

Snowcone (8TB), Snowball Edge (80TB), Snowmobile (100PB truck). Physical devices for massive offline data transfer.

Example: Migrating 50TB of video footage to AWS using a Snowball Edge device shipped overnight instead of weeks of uploads.

🗂Database Services

Database

Amazon RDS

Relational Database Service. Managed SQL databases: MySQL, PostgreSQL, MariaDB, Oracle, SQL Server. AWS handles patching, backups, scaling.

Example: Running a MySQL database for a web app without managing OS patches, backups, or replication yourself.
Database

Amazon Aurora

Cloud-native relational DB compatible with MySQL and PostgreSQL. Up to 5x faster than MySQL, 3x faster than PostgreSQL. Auto-scales storage.

Example: A high-traffic SaaS platform needing MySQL compatibility but with better performance and automatic failover.
Database

Amazon DynamoDB

Fully managed NoSQL key-value and document database. Single-digit millisecond latency at any scale. Serverless with auto-scaling.

Example: Storing user session data for a mobile app that needs <5ms reads across millions of users.
Database

Amazon Redshift

Fully managed data warehouse. Run complex SQL analytics on petabytes of structured data. Columnar storage for fast aggregation.

Example: A retail company analyzing 3 years of sales data to find purchasing trends across regions.
Database

Amazon ElastiCache

Managed in-memory caching (Redis or Memcached). Sub-millisecond response times for frequently accessed data.

Example: Caching database query results so repeated requests are served from memory instead of hitting the database.
Database

Amazon DocumentDB

Managed document database compatible with MongoDB. Scalable JSON document storage.

Example: A content management system storing articles and metadata as JSON documents with flexible schemas.
Database

Amazon Neptune

Managed graph database for highly connected datasets. Supports property graphs and RDF.

Example: Building a social network feature showing "friends of friends" or recommendation engines.

🌐Networking & Content Delivery

Networking

Amazon VPC

Virtual Private Cloud. Your own logically isolated network in AWS. Control IP ranges, subnets, route tables, internet gateways, and security.

Example: Creating a private subnet for databases (no internet access) and a public subnet for web servers.
Networking

Amazon CloudFront

Global CDN (Content Delivery Network). Caches content at 400+ Edge Locations worldwide for low-latency delivery.

Example: Serving a website's images and videos from the nearest Edge Location instead of a single origin server.
Networking

Amazon Route 53

Scalable DNS web service. Translates domain names to IP addresses. Also handles domain registration and health checks.

Example: Routing "myapp.com" to your load balancer, with failover to a backup region if the primary goes down.
Networking

Elastic Load Balancing (ELB)

Distributes incoming traffic across multiple targets. Types: Application LB (HTTP/Layer 7), Network LB (TCP/Layer 4), Gateway LB.

Example: Spreading web requests evenly across 5 EC2 instances so no single server gets overloaded.
Networking

AWS Direct Connect

Dedicated private network connection from your data center to AWS. Bypasses the public internet for consistent performance.

Example: A bank with strict compliance needs a private 10Gbps line from their office to AWS instead of using the internet.
Networking

AWS Global Accelerator

Directs traffic to optimal endpoints via the AWS global network. Improves availability and performance for global users.

Example: Users in Europe are automatically routed to the closest healthy endpoint, cutting latency by 60%.
Networking

AWS VPN

Encrypted connection between your on-premises network and your VPC over the public internet. Cheaper alternative to Direct Connect.

Example: A small office securely connecting to their AWS VPC over an encrypted tunnel for $0.05/hr.

🔒Security, Identity & Compliance

Security

AWS IAM

Identity and Access Management. Create Users, Groups, Roles, and Policies to control who can access what. Free to use.

Example: Creating a "Developers" group with permission to deploy code but not delete databases.
Security

IAM Roles

Temporary credentials for services or users. No permanent password/keys. Can be assumed by EC2, Lambda, or cross-account users.

Example: An EC2 instance assumes a role to read from S3 — no access keys stored on the server.
Security

AWS CloudTrail

Logs all API calls and account activity across your AWS infrastructure. Essential for auditing and compliance.

Example: Investigating who deleted an S3 bucket last Tuesday by searching CloudTrail logs.
Security

Amazon GuardDuty

Intelligent threat detection using ML and anomaly detection. Monitors VPC Flow Logs, CloudTrail, and DNS logs continuously.

Example: GuardDuty alerts you that an EC2 instance is communicating with a known cryptocurrency mining IP.
Security

AWS Shield

Managed DDoS protection. Standard = free, automatic. Advanced = paid, enhanced protection + 24/7 response team.

Example: Shield Standard automatically mitigates a volumetric DDoS attack on your website without you doing anything.
Security

AWS WAF

Web Application Firewall. Filter HTTP/HTTPS requests with custom rules. Block SQL injection, XSS, and bot traffic.

Example: Blocking all requests from specific countries or requests containing SQL injection patterns.
Security

AWS KMS

Key Management Service. Create and manage encryption keys. Integrated with S3, EBS, RDS, etc. for encryption at rest.

Example: Encrypting all S3 objects with a KMS key so even if someone accesses the bucket, data is unreadable.
Security

AWS Config

Records and evaluates resource configurations over time. Set rules to check compliance automatically.

Example: A rule that alerts you whenever an S3 bucket is made public, flagging it as non-compliant.
Security

Amazon Inspector

Automated vulnerability scanning for EC2 instances and container images. Checks for CVEs and network exposure.

Example: Inspector finds that your EC2 instance has an unpatched Apache vulnerability and flags it as critical.
Security

Amazon Macie

Uses ML to discover, classify, and protect sensitive data (PII, financial data) stored in S3.

Example: Macie finds credit card numbers accidentally stored in an S3 bucket and alerts the security team.
Security

AWS Secrets Manager

Store, rotate, and manage secrets (database passwords, API keys). Automatic rotation supported.

Example: Your app retrieves its database password from Secrets Manager at runtime instead of hardcoding it.
Security

Security Groups vs NACLs

Security Groups: instance-level firewall, stateful, allow rules only. NACLs: subnet-level, stateless, allow + deny rules.

Example: Security group allows port 443 inbound to a web server. NACL blocks a specific IP range at the subnet level.

🛠Management & Governance

Management

Amazon CloudWatch

Monitoring and observability service. Collects metrics, logs, and events. Set alarms and dashboards for your resources.

Example: An alarm triggers when CPU usage exceeds 80% for 5 minutes, sending you an email notification.
Management

AWS CloudFormation

Infrastructure as Code (IaC). Define resources in JSON/YAML templates. Deploy, update, and delete as "stacks."

Example: A template that creates a VPC, subnets, EC2 instances, and an RDS database in one click — repeatable across environments.
Management

AWS Systems Manager

Manage EC2 instances at scale. Patch management, run commands, parameter store for config values, session manager for SSH-free access.

Example: Patching 200 EC2 instances at once during a maintenance window without logging into each one.
Management

AWS Trusted Advisor

Real-time best practice checks across 5 categories: cost optimization, performance, security, fault tolerance, service limits.

Example: Trusted Advisor flags that you have idle EC2 instances costing $300/month and recommends stopping them.
Management

AWS Organizations

Centrally manage multiple AWS accounts. Apply policies (SCPs) across accounts. Consolidated billing for volume discounts.

Example: A company creates separate accounts for Dev, Staging, and Prod, governed by a central policy that prevents anyone from disabling CloudTrail.
Management

AWS Auto Scaling

Automatically adjusts capacity based on demand. Scales EC2 instances, DynamoDB tables, Aurora replicas, etc.

Example: Scaling from 2 to 8 EC2 instances during peak hours and back to 2 at night.
Management

AWS Control Tower

Set up and govern a secure, multi-account AWS environment based on best practices. Automates account setup with guardrails.

Example: Automatically creating new team accounts with pre-configured security guardrails and logging.
Management

AWS Well-Architected Tool

Review your workloads against the 6 pillars. Identifies high-risk areas and provides improvement recommendations.

Example: Running a review that flags your app has no Multi-AZ deployment — a reliability risk.

🔗Application Integration & Developer Tools

Integration

Amazon SQS

Simple Queue Service. Fully managed message queue for decoupling microservices. Messages wait in the queue until processed.

Example: An order system puts messages in SQS; a separate payment service processes them at its own pace.
Integration

Amazon SNS

Simple Notification Service. Pub/sub messaging — one message sent to many subscribers (email, SMS, Lambda, SQS, HTTP).

Example: When a deploy finishes, SNS sends an email to the team + triggers a Lambda for post-deploy checks.
Integration

Amazon EventBridge

Serverless event bus. Route events from AWS services, SaaS apps, or custom apps to targets like Lambda, SQS, Step Functions.

Example: Every time an EC2 instance changes state, EventBridge triggers a Lambda to update a dashboard.
Integration

AWS Step Functions

Orchestrate multiple AWS services into serverless workflows using visual state machines.

Example: A multi-step order pipeline: validate payment → reserve inventory → send confirmation → ship.
Integration

AWS CodePipeline

Fully managed CI/CD service. Automates build, test, and deploy phases every time there's a code change.

Example: Pushing code to GitHub automatically triggers a build, runs tests, and deploys to production.
Integration

AWS CodeDeploy

Automates code deployment to EC2, Lambda, or on-premises servers. Supports rolling, blue/green, and canary deployments.

Example: Deploying a new version to 10% of servers first (canary), then rolling to the rest if no errors.
Integration

Amazon API Gateway

Managed service to create, publish, and secure REST/WebSocket APIs at any scale. Handles throttling, auth, and caching.

Example: Exposing a Lambda function as a public REST API endpoint with rate limiting and API key authentication.

💰Billing, Pricing & Support

Billing

AWS Free Tier

Three types: Always Free (Lambda 1M requests/mo), 12 Months Free (EC2 t2.micro 750hrs/mo), Trials (short-term for specific services).

Example: New accounts get 750 hours of EC2 t2.micro per month for a year — enough to run one instance 24/7 for free.
Billing

EC2 Pricing Models

On-Demand (pay/sec, no commitment), Reserved (1-3yr, up to 72% off), Spot (up to 90% off, interruptible), Savings Plans (flexible commitment), Dedicated Hosts (physical server).

Example: Use Reserved for your always-on production DB, Spot for batch processing that can retry, On-Demand for unpredictable dev work.
Billing

AWS Cost Explorer

Visualize, understand, and manage costs over time. Graphs, filters, forecasting, and Savings Plans recommendations.

Example: Discovering that 40% of your spend is on idle dev instances running overnight.
Billing

AWS Budgets

Set custom cost/usage budgets and get alerts (email or SNS) when you exceed or are forecasted to exceed thresholds.

Example: Setting a $500/month budget with alerts at 80% and 100% thresholds.
Billing

AWS Pricing Calculator

Web tool to estimate costs before deploying. Build architecture estimates to plan budgets.

Example: Estimating that your planned 3-tier app will cost ~$350/month before actually provisioning anything.
Billing

Consolidated Billing

Feature of AWS Organizations. One bill for multiple accounts + volume discounts since usage is aggregated.

Example: Three team accounts collectively use 500TB of S3 and hit a cheaper pricing tier together.
Billing

Cost Allocation Tags

Key-value pairs on resources to categorize costs. Appear in billing reports for tracking by project, team, or environment.

Example: Tagging all resources with "Project: MobileApp" so you can filter billing to see that project's exact spend.
Billing

AWS Support Plans

Basic (free, docs/forums), Developer ($29/mo, email support), Business ($100+/mo, 24/7 phone + all Trusted Advisor), Enterprise ($15K+/mo, TAM + Concierge).

Example: Enterprise plan gives you a dedicated Technical Account Manager who proactively reviews your architecture.

💡Key Concepts to Remember

Concept

Regions & Availability Zones

Region = geographic location (us-east-1). AZ = isolated data center(s) within a Region (us-east-1a). Deploy across 2+ AZs for high availability.

Example: Your app runs in us-east-1a and us-east-1b. If one AZ has an outage, the other keeps serving traffic.
Concept

Edge Locations

Sites used by CloudFront and Route 53 to cache content close to users. There are more Edge Locations than Regions (400+).

Example: A user in Tokyo gets cached content from a Tokyo Edge Location instead of the origin server in Virginia.
Concept

Shared Responsibility Model

AWS = security OF the cloud (hardware, network, facilities, hypervisor). Customer = security IN the cloud (data, OS, firewall rules, IAM, encryption).

Example: AWS secures the physical server. You secure what runs on it — OS patches, your app's code, and who can access your data.
Concept

IaaS vs PaaS vs SaaS

IaaS (EC2) = you manage OS up. PaaS (Beanstalk) = you manage code only. SaaS (Gmail) = vendor manages everything.

Example: EC2 = you patch the OS. Beanstalk = you just deploy code. Rekognition = you just call the API.
Concept

Elasticity vs Scalability

Elasticity = automatic scale up AND down with demand. Scalability = ability to handle growth (may be manual). Elasticity implies automation.

Example: Auto Scaling = elasticity (automatic). Adding a bigger instance manually = scalability.
Concept

High Availability vs Fault Tolerance

HA = system stays accessible (may have brief interruption). FT = zero downtime even during failures (more expensive).

Example: Multi-AZ RDS = high availability (failover in ~60s). An active-active setup with no downtime = fault tolerant.
